# PAUL 23-01 — Input responsiveness and truth-in-rendering for Table Soccer

**Date:** 2026-11-20. **Baseline:** `49ca0360504dfed4bb6f6f47782111ad91fe001d`, root Foosball. **Branch:** `fix/foosball-direct-rods-crisp-table-soccer`.
**User evidence:** screenshot shows “NEON GLASS / TABLE SOCCER 2.5D” with translucent double silhouettes and apparent offset strike-face bars; human reports unresponsive rod input.

## SEED — Observed / derived / decided / not verified
- **OBSERVED (code):** Table Soccer ZIP mesh is baked into 9 independent pose PNGs per 8 rods, not a runtime skinned 3D model. `mesh_art.lua` cross-fades 2 full-body frames, making see-through/ghost silhouettes; the static table is drawn with just 0.12 alpha.
- **OBSERVED (code):** mouse sets *targets* and `stepRod` uses critically damped low-acceleration springs (translation k=120, damping 19; rotation k=180, damping 20) for **human and AI alike**. At fixed 120 Hz this is consistent but can feel sluggish. In AUTO, the rod may change near ball transitions.
- **DECIDED:** keep authoritative 2D simulation and 2-microstep collision accuracy. Human rods follow targets via a bounded, short-timescale **velocity servo** computed during the fixed step, with physical `vy/omega` still used for ball impulses and mechanical bumpers. AI keeps the existing damped controller.
- **DECIDED:** visual-only fix: avoid alpha blending two silhouettes; choose one crisp pose. Optional new **25-pose** private local baking reduces angular stepping, while already generated **9-pose** packs still load automatically. Raise table reference visibility moderately and clarify that these are 2.5D *pre-rendered sprites*, not runtime 3D meshes.
- **DECIDED:** no third-party original geometry or derivative images uploaded to Git; local baker only. Do not touch the frozen 11vs11.
- **UNVERIFIED:** whether ~40–80ms human-rod response feels best on user's mouse; visual alignment of approximate 2.5D footprint; on-device FPS. Requires human UAT.

## PAUL acceptance criteria
1. **AC1 physical response:** target angle/slide respond much faster within bounded velocity per microstep, without instantly teleporting, overshooting end stops, or losing real impulse contact. AI dynamics unchanged; old physics regression retained.
2. **AC2 input & AUTO:** deliberate mouse movement holds the selected rod briefly; TAB and 1–4/manual lock intact; no new complex control scheme.
3. **AC3 2.5D clarity:** one opaque nearest-angle sprite per rod (no double vision), optional 25 independently baked poses, 9-pose backwards compatibility, coherent selection/strike edge overlay, still fallback to procedural graphics.
4. **AC4 regressions:** add response/asset tests for Lua 5.4 + LuaJIT and baker smoke; both Foosball and frozen NEON11 jobs green.
5. **AC5 manual:** at least 10 deliberate shots, compare AUTO/MANUALE and 2.5D/procedural; inspect collider lines with F1; measure native response and look, not claimed by CI.

## Game Design Lab reality check
**Hypothesis:** lowering *control input-to-pose* lag makes deliberate hits more predictable and increases skill expression. Reducing multi-pose ghosting makes the displayed striking edge easier to perceive. Potential failure: faster foot sweeps are too powerful, or nearest-pose stepping becomes noticeable. **Stop rule:** prefer a tunable 9-pose-compatible low-impact solution; if either visual or physical side regresses, isolate and roll back the change instead of adding menus or 3D physics.
