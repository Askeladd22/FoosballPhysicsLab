# PAUL 30-01 — Real 3D foosball: see the table LEGS below the playing field

**Date:** 2026-11-20. **Branch:** `fix/foosball-show-real-legs-3quarter-camera`. **Base:** `7292fceddf761bf5fa51a1284732dca2a6d45cbe`.

## Evidence and diagnosis (SEED)
- **Observed:** repeated user Windows screenshots show only the green pitch, black rim and rods seen almost straight down; the lower body and the actual four legs are consistently **off-screen**. The previous PAUL 29 camera targets `(500,15,280)` and looks almost vertically down from `Y=1300`. It therefore **cannot show the actual 3D foosball as a table with legs**.
- **Grounded in real source:** the public CC BY GLB's `table.bin` has supports at **Y=-740**, field surface about **Y=-4**, raised rim up to **Y=130**. The meshes are correctly upright. Prior horizontal 180° yaw works; **vertical flipping is wrong**.
- **Visual experiment actually run against the user's converted 11 real meshes:** a CPU-side VTK 3D renderer (no arbitrary reimagining) at a **three-quarter corner camera**, looking at the **FULL object center Y=-305**, shows four support legs below the pitch as requested. In high-angle corner pose (reference image `/mnt/data/real_corner_high.png`) the field, full body, and four feet all appear clearly together.
- **Root cause:** all previous cameras centered the target on the *field*, not on the full mesh bounds Y=-740..130; the composition pushed legs beyond the 3D viewport. Repeating pixel-green tests cannot prove support visibility. Prior camera tests even mandated steep elevation >75°, *preventing* 3/4 tabletop framing.

## PLAN / acceptance
1. **AC1 — full-body camera**: default perspective in `renderer3d.lua` looks at `(500,-305,280)` with a real 3/4 corner viewpoint (approximately X=-512, Y=1814, Z=-1515 after calibration) and FOV35, with aspect-adaptive safety. Preserve F2 opposite-side, PageUp/PageDown zoom, Home reset, P/V real-3D/2D toggle, F3 optional 180° yaw.
2. **AC2 — legs under in screen, field above**: add a pure Lua camera test at 900×650, 1280×850, 1650×895, 1920×1080 checking *all four world leg-bottom anchors* within the world Canvas and **projected beneath** the field/pitch; full rim and pitch remain visible. Assert a three-quarter elevation **30–60°**, not a top-down 79° camera. No 180° roll or X-axis flip of source meshes.
3. **AC3 — evidence-based native GPU test**: retain non-black and green-field tests on the **actual versioned CC BY GLB**; add a leg-visibility/readability test for actual support pixels in the lower half of the scene. A passing code test is not enough; separate actual rendered visual reference and player UAT.
4. **AC4 — scope safety**: no physics, AI, rod collision, original CC BY geometry, GLB file, or frozen `legacy/neon11/` modification. Do not introduce g3d; existing geometry pipeline already renders true 3D.
5. **AC5 — handoff**: a native Windows screenshot of the new full-table camera, and ten intentional mouse shots, remain the game-design human acceptance gate.

## SEED decision log
**DECIDED:** render the actual full model with four support feet within the viewport; retain its world-Y upright geometry. **ASSUMED:** corner 3/4 view from the near-left corner matches the user's demand for an obviously upright table. **OPEN:** subjective playability and ease of judging ball/foot contacts at the player's preferred zoom; g3d migration not justified.

## Game Design Lab — decisive comparison
A frame showing **four visible legs BELOW the table while field and ball remain visible** is mandatory. Reject a camera even if 100% of green-field pixels pass when the support legs are outside the viewport. Only after this test pass decide camera polish or physical tuning.
